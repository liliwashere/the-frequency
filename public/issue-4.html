Gergely Orosz · pragmaticengineer.com · open.substack.com
The impact of AI on software engineers in 2026: key trends. Part 1
A 900-respondent survey of software engineers finds AI tools have sharply uneven effects: "Shippers" who focus on task completion gain the most, while "Builders" doing large-scale work report more AI slop and mounting cost concerns. Around 30% of respondents have already hit usage limits on their AI tooling.

arstechnica.com
Hackers duped Meta AI support chatbot to steal celebrity Instagram accounts - Ars Technica
Meta's AI support chatbot was exploited to steal high-value Instagram accounts by simply asking it to change the target account's email address — no 2FA required, just a matching VPN location. Meta patched it on May 29, but the incident illustrates the concrete security risk of granting agent-level permissions to customer-facing LLMs.

arstechnica.com
Trump loses more control over AI regulation as Illinois passes landmark law - Ars Technica
Illinois passed the strongest state-level AI safety law in the US, requiring frontier AI labs to publish safety plans, submit to independent third-party testing, and report critical incidents within 24–72 hours — days after the Trump administration dropped its own federal oversight plan. The law includes whistleblower protections and is now awaiting the governor's signature.

Rina Chandran · restofworld.org · restofworld.org
Asia is excited about AI, the U.S. not so much - Rest of World
New survey data shows 84% of Chinese respondents are excited about AI products versus only 38% of Americans — and Singapore's adoption rate (61%) is more than double the US figure. The trust gap extends to regulators, and the US is also losing AI researchers: emigration is up, immigration down 80% in the last year.

Johannes Klingebiel · klingebeil.substack.com · klingebeil.substack.com
Drei Tipps für “KI” in 2026 - by Johannes Klingebiel - Zine
Johannes Klingebiel argues the productivity promise of AI remains unproven at the macro level: enterprise gains are nearly invisible in GDP data, software developer demand is growing slowly, and benchmark "exponential" curves hide that 80%-success thresholds tell a far more sober story than the 50% ones typically cited. A grounded corrective to the hype cycle.
Originally in GermanDespite all predictions to the contrary, productivity gains at the enterprise level remain largely absent. AI's contribution to US GDP growth was more or less zero, and demand for software developers is growing, albeit slowly and more slowly than the graduation rate of students. The graphs tell a simple story: models show exponential capability development — provided you ignore the error bars and the fact that the graphs only measure whether systems succeeded in 50% of attempts. At 80%, the picture looks considerably more sober.

iaethiqueinsider.substack.com
🪢 IA & société : que dire à nos jeunes enfants, à nos ados et à nos jeunes diplômés ?
Eric Schmidt's May 2026 Arizona commencement speech was met with student protests — and this French-language essay unpacks why: graduates see AI transformation framed as inevitable, with empathy notably absent and a clear subtext of "adapt or be left behind." The author draws a parallel to climate change, where the burden of consequence falls on the generation least responsible for creating it.
Originally in FrenchOn May 15, 2026, Eric Schmidt took the stage at the University of Arizona to deliver a commencement address. His speech compared the AI revolution to that of the personal computer, warned that the transformation to come would be 'broader, faster, and more consequential than anything we have seen before,' and touched every profession, classroom, hospital, and relationship. Beneath the elegant phrasing, however, was a glaring lack of empathy and a fairly clear subtext: you have no choice, so figure it out — and in the end you'll see that AI isn't so bad.

Alexey · alexeyondata.substack.com · alexeyondata.substack.com
What 1,000+ Job Descriptions Reveal About the AI Engineer Role in 2026
An analysis of 1,000+ real job postings finds that 70% of AI engineer roles are directly building LLM systems (RAG, agents, evals), while 28.5% focus on surrounding infrastructure — and Python fluency appears in 82.5% of listings. The data challenges the vague definitions circulating in the discourse and shows a role that is fundamentally a production-focused application engineer.

Shirin Khosravi Jam · jamwithai.substack.com · jamwithai.substack.com
Data Science Roadmap 2026 - by Shirin Khosravi Jam
Shirin Khosravi Jam draws on five years of classical data science before she touched LLMs to argue the real path to shipping ML systems is software engineering first — data cleaning, pipelines, and debugging come before neural nets. Her roadmap is built around her own career arc, not a syllabus.

swyx and Alessio interview the engineers actually building frontier systems — not the comms teams. 175+ episodes, zero fluff. Their AI for Science arc and the Claude Code Anonymous episode are essential listening for anyone shipping with LLMs.
⏱ ~60 min · AI engineers + builders · 175+ eps

How I AI · Claire Vo · Lenny's spinoff
Real AI workflows, live screen shares — the format every podcast should steal
30-minute episodes with practitioners showing their actual AI setup on screen. No scripted hot takes — just what people actually do day to day. Best for product people and builders who learn by watching, not reading.
~30 min · Product + builder audience

No Priors · Elad Gil & Sarah Guo
The most technically honest AI investor podcast
Elad Gil and Sarah Guo don't softball. Episodes with Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross, and leading researchers speak plainly about what AI actually can and can't do. No "AI will change everything" non-answers — just sharp investor-grade thinking.
~45 min · Founders + researchers

TWIML AI Podcast · Sam Charrington
Deep technical interviews with ML researchers and practitioners
Sam Charrington has been running TWIML (This Week in Machine Learning & AI) since 2016 — one of the longest-running ML podcasts with serious technical depth. Best for following research trends: papers, benchmarks, and practitioner case studies that don't make headlines.
