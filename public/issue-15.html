✦ Must read
arstechnica.com
Suspecting court of using AI, man injected prompts in filings to try to win case - Ars Technica
A US plaintiff hid prompt-injection text in court filings, attempting to manipulate an AI system he suspected the court was using to review cases — the first documented instance of this attack in US litigation. The judge called the hidden text evidence of malicious intent and noted a parallel case in Brazil where attorneys used the same tactic against a court AI.

Quick hits
wired.com
AI Could Help Fossil Fuel Companies Create More Emissions - wired.com
A peer-reviewed study in npj Climate Action — co-authored by former Microsoft sustainability workers who quit over the company's oil-and-gas contracts — finds AI productivity gains in the fossil fuel sector could add greenhouse gas emissions equivalent to Russia's annual output at the high end. The finding reframes the AI-climate debate: data center energy draw is small compared to what AI-enabled upstream oil and gas production could release.
Research Read →
wired.com
There’s a Fatty Liver Epidemic. AI Could Help Get Ahead of It - WIRED
Over a billion people worldwide have fatty liver disease, but routine noninvasive screening is rarely deployed even for high-risk patients; researchers argue AI mining of electronic health records could close that gap. The piece cites CUNY's Jeffrey Lazarus on why the bottleneck is clinical workflow adoption, not detection accuracy.
Research Read →
techcabal.com
Ghana & Kenya’s tech edge: Can Africa lead in Ethical AI? | TechCabal
Ghana and Kenya are building AI governance frameworks grounded in community and fairness at a moment when Silicon Valley and Beijing are mired in bias and surveillance scandals — but digital colonialism looms: subsea cables, cloud infrastructure, and research agendas are still controlled by Google, Meta, and AWS. The piece argues Africa's youthful, diverse societies could export an alternative model for ethical AI if they can retain control of the value chain.
AI × Business Read →
huggingface.co
Paper page - Empirical Privacy Evaluations of Generative and Predictive Machine Learning Models -- A review and challenges for practice
This review of empirical privacy evaluation methods for generative and predictive ML models finds a fundamental trade-off: methods that are computationally feasible at scale (millions of training records) tend to assume adversaries that are unrealistically capable for most real-world deployments. For practitioners in healthcare and statistical agencies, this means current privacy auditing tools may give false confidence.
Research Read →
huggingface.co
Paper page - Data and its (dis)contents: A survey of dataset development and use in machine learning research
A broad survey of dataset development and use in ML research finds that the ways datasets are collected, constructed, and shared actively shape which problems the field pursues and which methods it explores — and that predominant practices carry significant practical and ethical risks. The authors call for a more cautious, thorough understanding of data as a first-class research artifact.
Research Read →
arstechnica.com
The new Instagram logo is the perfect embodiment of AI slop - Ars Technica
The new Instagram wordmark — described by its own head of product as 'leaner and more modern' — looks uncannily like the malformed text generative AI produces in images: a cutoff 's', mismatched typefaces, letters that almost-but-don't-quite cohere. The piece uses the logo as a lens for what happens when AI aesthetics leak into brand design without any human editor catching it.

Nicholas · Not Another CEO · notanotherceo.substack.com
What I learned as CEO of a failed SaaS company (that every AI founder should hear)
The CEO of Workstream.io writes post-mortem essays from the moment of shutdown, when the lessons were 'most raw': the core finding is that nothing compensates for a product that isn't solving a customer's top-priority problem, and that a 10x improvement over alternatives is the minimum bar for a must-have product. Written in the first person with specific decisions, not generic startup wisdom.

Lessons learned from 10 years of applied research in a fast growing startup
Pedro Bizarro, a co-founder navigating a decade of applied research inside a fast-growing startup, writes about the friction between research timelines and business pressure — capturing the tension between long-term thinking and short-term survival from someone who actually lived it.
