Editor's pick
✦ Must read
Wired Staff · wired.com
Claude Helped a Hacker Find a Way to Issue Tickets to Almost Every US Music Festival - WIRED
Security researcher Ian Carroll used Claude Opus 4.7 to discover a vulnerability in Front Gate Tickets — the backend for Lollapalooza, SXSW, and virtually every major US festival — that let him freely issue tickets of any value to anyone. The finding is a concrete, documented case of AI-assisted offensive security working at scale against live production infrastructure.

Quick hits
Ars Technica · arstechnica.com
Google’s AI buildout drove 37% increase in electricity use in 2025 - Ars Technica
Google's 2025 sustainability report reveals a 37% single-year jump in electricity consumption — the largest in company history — driven by AI data center expansion, with total usage up 250% since 2019. Supply-chain emissions from Asia-Pacific manufacturers rose 25% separately, widening the gap between Google's clean-energy commitments and its actual carbon footprint.

Ars Technica · arstechnica.com
After spooking Trump into safety testing, Anthropic AI models get global release - Ars Technica
The Trump administration lifted export controls on Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models after Anthropic committed to proactive security monitoring and coordinated government protocols — a roughly three-week hold that set a new precedent for AI model export regulation. The Glasswing program, giving cybersecurity researchers access to Mythos for defensive use, is now expanding internationally.

technologyreview.com
Agriculture is ready for AI, but its data isn’t - MIT Technology Review
AI vendors are selling precision-agriculture solutions that depend on data foundations most farms don't have — field-level heterogeneity, incomplete sensor coverage, and messy historical records mean models produce confident-sounding recommendations built on bad inputs. The piece is a practitioner warning aimed at agriculture executives, arguing data infrastructure must precede AI investment.

How Polsia builds and runs companies with AI agents
Ben Broca's Polsia runs multiple businesses with zero employees by orchestrating AI agents that handle 80% of operations autonomously — targeting non-technical founders who want a fully autonomous system rather than tools they have to operate. AI Now Institute · ainowinstitute.org
[PDF] ARTIFICIAL POWER - AI Now Institute
The AI Now Institute's 2025 Landscape Report maps how concentrated AI power in a handful of corporations is reshaping labor, healthcare, criminal justice, and democratic governance — with contributions from researchers including Timnit Gebru and Abeba Birhane. The report argues that AI's societal harms are structural and deliberate, not accidental side effects of fast-moving technology.

Linas · linas.substack.com · linas.substack.com
Anthropic Just Told AI Founders What to Build in 2026
Anthropic's analysis of 1 million Claude conversations found that ~6% involved people seeking genuine personal guidance on health, finances, relationships, and legal matters — overwhelmingly because they couldn't afford professional alternatives. The piece uses Cal AI's trajectory ($0 to $50M ARR with 7 employees, acquired by MyFitnessPal) to argue this data signals a specific, undercrowded product category.

Partnership on AI · partnershiponai.org
Prioritizing Equity in Algorithmic Systems through Inclusive Data Guidelines - Partnership on AI
Partnership on AI's Participatory & Inclusive Demographic Data Guidelines address the core tension in algorithmic fairness work: collecting demographic data to detect bias creates its own harms for the populations most at risk. The framework offers practitioners concrete guidance on how to structure data collection with affected communities rather than on them.
